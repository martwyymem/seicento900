# Seicento 900 Cup — Godot port (work in progress)

Godot 4.7, GDScript. The JS game in the repo root stays as the reference until this version matches it.

Run `godot --path godot` to open the front end. The set-up page covers lap and timed (endurance) races on
all eleven circuits, opponents and difficulty, weather, start time, grid position, driving aids, gearbox,
driver profile and paint, pit rules, tyre wear, fuel use, crew, AI learning and bot physics. As in the
reference, the strategist lists the fastest pit plans for those settings (lap time, fuel per lap, pit-stop
cost and tyre life per compound) and a stint editor validates hand-built plans; the chosen plan is what the
race starts with. Circuits apply their default race length and tyre wear, endurance turns refuelling on,
the page remembers its settings between sessions and shows what the learning AI knows about you on the
selected circuit (with a Forget button). Championship mode shows the calendar with progress and the saved
standings, plans each round automatically and scores F1-style points.

The race view has the procedural 3D circuit, a Fiat Seicento 900 built in Blender to the real car's dimensions (`models/seicento.glb`, generated by `tools/blender/build_seicento.py`) with lights, wheel spin/steer and body
roll, Blender-built grandstands with a seated crowd, pit garages in team colours, race control, a lattice start gantry and TV towers (`models/trackside.glb`, from `tools/blender/build_trackside.py`), a day/night sky, rain, headlight beams, skid marks, start lights and eight cameras (chase, far chase,
CMR's bumper and bonnet cams, roof, trackside TV, bird's-eye, full track). The whole interface copies Colin McRae Rally 2.0's layout, recoloured in purples and oranges
(`scripts/ui/cmr_style.gd` holds the palette and theme; the type is the bundled Nunito Sans, OFL, in
`fonts/`). The front end uses CMR's flat page tints, thin text tabs split by hairlines, the big sliding
word strip under a line drawing of the circuit on the main menu, pale flat fields, and the
information and repair layouts (map box, weather column, gradient bar, parts list with status marks). In
the race the HUD shows CMR's fading standings bars, the co-driver's pace-note boxes (see below), split
times, a segmented lap-progress bar and clock, an event feed, a pit-wall panel (tyres, fuel, plan,
next stop), a curved segmented rev band with a big gear digit, a minimap, name tags and a help overlay.
There is a pause menu, time warp with co-driver, skip-to-results after retiring or finishing, and a
results screen with championship points.

## Race control

Race control (`scripts/sim/race_control.gd`, on by default; set-up page → Race control) is a Godot-only
addition that the JS reference doesn't have. It acts after the contact physics:

- **Damage.** Hard hits on cars or barriers bend the car: it loses power, gains drag and pulls to one side.
  The pit wall shows the damage and calls the car in once it passes 30%; repairs add to the stop time. One
  big enough impact wrecks the car and it slides to rest where it hit. Bots are damaged the same way (a gauge
  beside each name in the timing tower, darker paint) and limp round to the pits. A bot that spins drives
  itself round to face the right way again rather than being lifted back onto the track.
- **Stewards.** Every car-to-car contact is investigated. Whoever drove into the other car gets a warning or a
  5/10 s time penalty, depending on the consequences, repeat offences and whether it happened under
  yellows or the safety car. A place taken under yellows or the safety car must be given back within 12 s. Going
  too fast for double yellows or the safety-car delta for more than a few seconds costs 5 s.
- **Yellow flags.** Each marshal sector is about 200 m. A spun, stopped or beached car brings out a yellow
  there and in the sector before it (double yellow if it's on the racing surface). Bots slow down and
  don't overtake there. With a car stranded anywhere the whole circuit is yellow and the field, the leader
  included, eases off; bots steer round wrecks lying on the track. **V** switches the flag speed limiter on: it holds your car under the speed the flags allow
  (you still steer and brake), so you can't be penalised for speeding. Steering round a wreck or a limping
  car is never counted as an illegal overtake.
- **Safety car.** It comes out when a car is stopped on the track or several are stranded. It leaves the pit
  lane ahead of the leader, or joins further round the lap if the leader is too far away. The field queues
  behind it while marshals recover the cars, and a strategist call offers a cheap stop. Once the queue has
  formed, it is announced as coming in, and the race goes green when the leader crosses the line. The whole
  grid closes up behind it, and bots re-plan: those due for a stop (or on worn tyres) take the cheap stop,
  and the leaders sometimes gamble on staying out.
- **Red flag.** A heavy barrier impact or a pile-up of three wrecks within 20 s stops the race. The field
  drives slowly back to the pit lane and queues at the exit. Crews then have 30 s to repair the cars and fit
  tyres for the conditions, and the race restarts behind the safety car.

The HUD shows the flag board under the pace notes, yellow sectors, wrecks and the safety car on the
minimap, damage and penalties on the pit wall, and race-control messages in the feed. With race control
off, the race behaves as the reference does, except that a spun bot drives itself round instead of being
lifted back onto the line; the parity tests turn that off too (`Race.bot_uturn`). To run the scenarios
headless: `godot --headless --path godot -s tests/smoke_rc.gd -- [clean|sc|red] [track] [laps]`.

## Live wear, fuel and pit calls

Set-up page → Live wear and stops (`RaceSettings.dynamic_running`, on by default; the parity tests turn it
off). It is a Godot-only addition:

- **Wear follows the load.** A bot's tyres now wear with the same load the player's do, from cornering and
  braking or accelerating against the grip available, so hard corners and traffic cost tread. Cold tyres,
  a heavy tank and each set's own quality (±8 %) change the rate too, so no two stints wear alike.
- **Fuel follows the drive.** Use was already tied to engine power; each car now also tracks what a lap
  really costs it, and refuels from that rather than the calibrated figure.
- **Stops follow the live state.** Every lap the car measures how much tread and fuel it really used
  (rises are believed quickly, falls slowly, so slow laps behind a safety car don't leave it short). If
  that drifts more than about 10 % from what the plan assumed, the plan is rebuilt from the real rates
  and the stop moves; a stop is also called when the tyres or tank can't cover another lap. About a
  third of the bots gamble on one lap past their plan when tyres and tank allow. The strategist tells you
  when your stop has moved.
- **The strategist advises, you decide.** Auto strategist is now off by default: it suggests the stop in
  the feed and you press **P**. Turn it on to have it box you on its own. A previously saved set-up keeps
  its old value.

## Slipstream

Set-up page → Slipstream (`RaceSettings.draft`, on by default; off in the parity tests). A car close behind
another one pulls less air: up to 35 % less drag right behind a car at speed, thinning over a few car
lengths (about 14 m per e-fold), narrowing to the width of the wake (about 1 m, a little wider further
back), and needing the car ahead to be moving faster than about 8 m/s. The tow builds over roughly 0.7 s
and is lost within 0.4 s of pulling out, so speed is gained and shed gradually. The same model tows bots and
the player; `Race.wake_strength` and `Race.tow_drag` hold it.

## Copied from Colin McRae Rally 2.0

Beyond the look, these work the way they do in CMR 2.0:

- **Pace notes and the co-driver.** `scripts/sim/pace_notes.gd` grades every corner from the racing line's
  curvature and the elevation, in CMR's words: flat, fast, easy, medium, tight, square or hairpin, left or
  right, then long, tightens, opens or over crest, "caution" where it bites, and "into", "and" or the
  distance (100, 150 … 500) to the next one. Crests on fast straights get their own note. Monza's first
  chicane comes out as "caution, square right into / square left, opens, 300". `scripts/ui/co_driver.gd`
  calls each note about 2.6 s before the corner and reads it aloud with the system's text-to-speech (a British
  English voice when one is installed; Options → Co-driver turns the voice off, M mutes it). The HUD draws
  the notes called and not yet driven as orange boxes, the arrow shaped for the grade (a U for a hairpin, an
  elbow for a square), with a red "!" tab for a caution and the call written underneath. There are no calls
  under time warp or in the pit lane, and the voice is quiet while the autopilot drives.
  `tests/_notes_probe.gd` prints a circuit's notes.
- **Split times.** Each lap is split in thirds. At each split a box beside the clock shows your time into the
  lap and the difference to the same split on your best lap of the session (red behind, purple ahead).
- **Damaged parts and the service area.** Race control's damage is now broken down into eight parts (body,
  suspension, steering, engine, gearbox, brakes, exhaust, electrics). Where the car was hit decides which
  parts take it: head-on hits reach the engine and steering, a hit from behind the exhaust and gearbox
  (`scripts/sim/service.gd`). The car still drives on the total. In a championship, the car leaves each
  round as it finished, and the Repair page becomes CMR's service area: 60 minutes of crew time, each part
  with its repair time, condition mark and line drawing. Select a part, then click it again or press Repair.
  Whatever isn't fixed starts the next round bent, with its power loss, drag and steering pull. A damaged
  car gets a Service Area button on the results. In a single race the car is rebuilt every time; the page
  only shows what the last race did to it.
- **Bumper and bonnet cameras.** C now steps through chase, far chase, bumper, bonnet, roof, trackside TV
  and bird's-eye.

## Lap records and ghost car

A Godot-only addition. `scripts/sim/lap_records.gd` keeps your best lap on each circuit, one for the dry and
one for the wet (a lap counts as wet when the track averages 25 % wetness or more over it), and records every
flying lap you drive yourself: from one crossing of the line to the next, with no autopilot, no pit stop and
the car on the tarmac all the way round (qualifying's track-limits rule). The lap from the grid and the
qualifying out lap never count. A faster lap replaces the record. The feed and the results announce it, and its
ghost (the car's path sampled every 0.05 s) is saved with it. Records are in `user://lap_records.json`, ghosts
in `user://ghosts/<track>_<dry|wet>.ghost`.

From then on a translucent copy of your car (`scripts/ui/lap_ghost.gd`) drives the record lap beside you on
every flying lap, fading further when it's on top of you. The clock box shows the live gap to the record at
your point on the lap (red behind, purple ahead), or the record itself before a flying lap starts. **G** shows
or hides the ghost. That setting is remembered separately for driving alone (qualifying, or a race without
opponents; shown by default) and for racing (hidden by default). The set-up page lists the circuit's records,
with a Forget button, and so does the Information page. `tests/_ghost_shot.gd` (run it in a window) records a
ghost on Fiat Track and screenshots the race, results and menus to `/tmp/ghost/`.

The UI is laid out on a 1600×900 canvas and scaled to the window (`canvas_items` stretch, `expand`
aspect), so it keeps its proportions at any resolution; the 3D views render at the window's full pixel
size. Options → Interface size makes the UI smaller or larger on top of that.

## Graphics and optional assets

The race world is rendered in Forward+ with shader-driven materials in `shaders/`: a sky with drifting
clouds, stars, sun and moon discs (`sky.gdshader`); asphalt with rubber on the racing line, patches, dusty
edges, drying lines, puddles and rain ripples (`road.gdshader`); terrain, grass and gravel that turn to rock on
steep slopes (`ground.gdshader`); wind-swayed trees (`foliage.gdshader`); harbour water (`water.gdshader`);
Monaco facades with shutters, shop fronts and lit windows at night (`facade.gdshader`); and grandstand crowds
(`crowd.gdshader`). Lighting uses AgX tonemapping, fog with aerial perspective, SSIL, glow and volumetric
fog in rain and at night.

Nothing has to be downloaded. `python3 tools/fetch_assets.py` (from the repo root) fetches CC0 HDRI skies
and PBR ground textures from Poly Haven into `godot/assets/` (gitignored). When they are present, the game
cross-fades the HDRIs with the time of day and uses the photo textures for close-up detail. Delete them to
go back to the procedural look. [`ASSETS.md`](ASSETS.md) lists where each file goes, other sources, tree models
and Asset Store add-ons. The quality preset (0 Low … 3 Ultra, default 2) is the `quality` key in the
`[graphics]` section of `user://graphics.cfg`.

### Upscaling and ray-traced reflections

Options → 3D upscaling offers AMD FSR 1 and FSR 2 and, on Apple GPUs, MetalFX Spatial and Temporal, at
100% (native anti-aliasing), 67%, 59%, 50% or 33% render resolution, plus FSR sharpening. The temporal
upscalers replace SMAA/FXAA, and MetalFX Temporal also turns MSAA off because it can't use it.

Options → Reflections picks Off, Screen-space (Godot's SSR) or Ray-traced. Godot 4.7's RenderingDevice
doesn't expose Metal's ray-tracing hardware (`SUPPORTS_RAY_QUERY` and acceleration structures are
Vulkan/D3D12-only), so the ray tracer runs in software on the GPU. `ray_tracing.gd` collects the circuit and cars into
triangle BVHs (`rt_bvh.gd`: one tree per mesh, a static top-level tree for the circuit and a per-frame one
for the cars). `rt_reflections.gd` is a CompositorEffect that runs `shaders/rt_reflections.glsl` after the
transparent pass. For each glossy pixel it traces the mirror ray, lights the hit with the sun (traced shadow
ray), the sky and emission, and puts it in place of the sky reflection Godot drew there, weighted by
Fresnel and roughness. Below Ultra quality it traces one ray per 2×2 block, from the block's glossiest pixel,
and shares it with the neighbouring pixels at the same depth. Pixels where the reflection would be too faint to
see aren't traced. The circuit's part of the BVH goes to the GPU once, and only the cars' part each frame. It costs
well under a millisecond at 1600×900 on an M4 Pro. `tests/_rt_shot.gd`
renders comparison screenshots and debug views: `DEBUG=1` traces primary rays, which should line up with
the rendered scene, and `DEBUG=2` shows only what the reflections see.

## Fiat Track

Fiat Track is Tor Fiata, Fiat Auto Poland's test circuit in Tychy (the "Motodrom" beside the factory). It is a Godot-only circuit. The centreline is way 143622821 in
OpenStreetMap, traced from the national orthophoto: 2.7 km clockwise, two ~185° right-handers of about 90 m
radius joined by straights of about 950 m and 1040 m, nearly flat (240–243 m). The road is 6.5 m wide, measured
on the orthophoto, and runs through scrub and pines with no grandstands. The published sources give no lap
length, corner names or start line, so those are estimates: the start/finish is on the north straight where the
infield roads join (the paddock area), the pit lane runs on the infield side, and the section names are
descriptive. The loop keeps the direction it is drawn in. The S-shaped bypass at the east end and the off-road
test areas in the infield (ramps, channels, bumps) are not part of the race circuit.

## Performance notes

**Battery and heat.** The frame rate is capped (Options → Frame rate limit: 30, 60 (default), 120 or unlimited);
without it a ProMotion display drives the game at 120 Hz. `Game._sync_rendering` renders the 3D view only while
it can be seen changing: it is off behind the opaque front-end pages and, when paused or on the results screen,
it draws a few more frames and then keeps its last image. In those states the cap drops to 30 fps, and to
10 fps while the window is in the background (which also pauses a running race). High no longer stacks MSAA on
top of SMAA and SSIL is Ultra-only; the sky's radiance cubemap refreshes only when the sky changes instead of
every frame. `tests/_perf.gd` is a frame-cost benchmark (`OLD=1` restores the previous MSAA/SSIL/sky settings for comparison).
The CPU floor is the 120 Hz simulation, about 2 ms per tick with 16 physics cars (mostly `Physics.step`), whatever the
frame rate. Set-up → Bot physics → Nearby only runs the physics just for bots within about 250–350 m of the player
(`Race.NEAR_PHYS_*`) and keeps the rest on the rail model; once the field spreads out that is about 0.75 ms per tick.

The race runs a fixed 120 Hz simulation with render interpolation and a spiral guard (at most 40 × warp
steps and ~40 ms of simulation per frame). The per-tick hot paths (`Physics.step`, `Race.traffic`,
`side_room`, `collide`, `Track.project`) avoid allocations and redundant work while keeping the arithmetic
identical to the reference: the physics parity tests still match to 1e-14 and fixed-seed races reproduce
bit for bit. Rough costs on an M4 Pro: 57 µs per car-tick of full physics, ~0.5 ms per tick for a
16-car field on rails (time warp / skipping).

## Layout

- `data/` — tunable resources you edit in the Inspector: `seicento.tres` (car), `tyres/*.tres` (compounds),
  `tracks/*.json` (centrelines + elevation generated from OpenStreetMap, all eleven circuits; rebuild with
  `python3 tools/build_tracks.py`, after `python3 tools/fetch_elevation.py <key>` for a new track).
- `scripts/data/` — resource classes (`CarSpec`, `TyreCompound`, `TrackDef`, `RaceSettings`).
- `scripts/sim/` — simulation, ported from `js/` (`CarModel`/`Tyres` ← car.js, `Physics`, `Track`,
  `Strategy`, `Race`, `Driver`, `Brain` (learning AI), `Weather` and helpers).
- `scripts/ui/` — `game.gd` (menus, set-up, race loop, input, pause, results, championship),
  `race_world.gd` (3D circuit, sky, cameras), `world_assets.gd` (optional skies, textures and tree models), `race_car_visual.gd`, `race_hud.gd`, `race_audio.gd`,
  `ray_tracing.gd`, `rt_bvh.gd` and `rt_reflections.gd` (ray-traced reflections).
- `ui/` — main game scene and race world scene.
- `tests/` — headless parity tests against the JS version.

Coordinates: the JS/2D x (east) and y (south) map to Godot X and Z; Y is up.

Keyboard: W/↑ throttle, S/↓/Space brake, A/D or ←/→ steer, E/Q shift (manual gearbox), P box this lap,
[ / ] tyre choice for the next stop, − / = fuel for the next stop, R re-plan, O autopilot, C camera,
L racing line, G ghost car, H help, M mute, , / . time warp, Esc pause, Enter skip to results once retired or finished.
Gamepad: left stick steers, triggers for throttle/brake, Start pauses.
